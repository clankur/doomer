GRPO vs PPO on ViZDoom

Published

May 30, 2026

GRPO became the default for LLM RL by dropping PPO’s critic in favor of a group-relative baseline: collect \(N\) episodes, assign each the advantage \((R_i - \bar{R}) / \sigma_R\). Having implemented PPO, the natural next question is whether that trade holds outside LLM post-training: does a critic-free baseline work in classical RL?

30+ runs on VizdoomBasic-v1 (RTX 4090, 10k episodes each). Answer: no. One GRPO run learned out of 30+ tested, and that result was not reproducible — even re-running with the same seed failed under GPU nondeterminism.

Setup

Environment. VizdoomBasic-v1: kill one enemy in a small room. Discrete(3) actions (left, right, attack). 84x84 grayscale, 4-frame stack, 300 max steps, frame_skip=4.

Network. Nature DQN CNN (3 conv layers, 512-dim FC). PPO adds a value head; GRPO removes it.

PPO config. GAE (\(\lambda=0.95\), \(\gamma=0.99\)), clipped surrogate (\(\epsilon=0.2\)), 4 PPO epochs, minibatch size 64.

GRPO config. Group size \(N=8\), same clipped surrogate and minibatch structure, no value function.

Running reward

PPO reaches positive reward by episode ~2,500 across all six hyperparameter configurations. GRPO baseline (flat episode-level advantages) flatlines at \(-160\). The single GRPO success (combined modifications, lr=5e-4, ec=0.02) reaches +50 but requires 2x the episodes and was the only working configuration out of 27 tested.

Entropy

PPO allows entropy to decay naturally as the policy converges. GRPO with low entropy coefficient (\(\leq 0.01\)) collapses to near-zero entropy without learning. The successful GRPO run (ec=0.02) maintained entropy above 0.2 during its learning phase.

Loss magnitude

PPO losses range from 0.1 to 1.0. GRPO losses sit at \(\approx -0.02\), two orders of magnitude smaller. The normalized return-to-go advantages have mean zero and unit variance; when averaged over a minibatch, positive and negative advantages cancel, producing near-zero policy loss. The entropy bonus (\(-c_e \cdot H\)) dominates, making total loss slightly negative.

Experiment grid

Four GRPO variants tested, each with a hyperparameter sweep. Total: 30+ runs.

Why GRPO fails here

Three structural problems, each confirmed by experiment:

1. No pretrained base model. GRPO replaces the value function with a group ranking. In LLM fine-tuning, the pretrained model is competent, so ranking completions is meaningful. A randomly initialized CNN produces garbage trajectories, yielding no signal.

2. Episode-level credit assignment. A 300-step episode requires per-step credit assignment. GRPO assigns the same advantage \(\hat{A}_i\) to every timestep in episode \(i\). Step 1 (spawn) and step 280 (aiming at enemy) receive identical gradient. PPO’s GAE computes per-step advantages via temporal differences:

\[\hat{A}_t = \sum_{l=0}^{T-t} (\gamma\lambda)^l \delta_{t+l}, \quad \delta_t = r_t + \gamma V(s_{t+1}) - V(s_t)\]

The return-to-go modification partially addresses this by computing \(G_t = \sum_{k=t}^{T} \gamma^{k-t} r_k\) per step. But without a learned baseline \(V(s_t)\), the advantages after normalization are dominated by noise.

3. Weak gradient signal. After group-relative normalization (mean 0, std 1), the policy loss \(-\mathbb{E}[\min(r_t \hat{A}_t, \text{clip}(r_t) \hat{A}_t)]\) averages near zero because positive and negative advantages cancel. Measured loss magnitude: \(\approx 0.02\) (GRPO) vs \(\approx 0.5\) (PPO). The value loss in PPO (\(\|V_\theta(s_t) - G_t\|^2\)) provides an additional gradient signal that stabilizes learning.

Seed reproducibility

The single GRPO success (exp 103, lr=5e-4, ec=0.02, seed=42) was retested with 4 additional seeds, plus a repeat of the original seed=42 to test determinism under GPU nondeterminism.

0/3 new seeds learned. The repeat of seed=42 (exp 120) also failed — GPU nondeterminism (cuDNN algorithm selection, floating-point operation ordering) causes trajectory divergence even with identical seeds. Exp 120 briefly exhibited the same entropy-drop pattern as 103 (entropy fell to 0.73 at group 380) but reverted to 1.37 instead of sustaining the drop that triggered 103’s learning.

The single success was a stochastic event: entropy happened to drop and stay low at the right time, causing the policy to commit to a viable strategy. This is not a learnable signal — it is a random walk that occasionally crosses a threshold.

Conclusion

PPO learned in 6/6 configurations. GRPO learned in 1/30+ runs, and that single success was not reproducible.

The critic GRPO discards is exactly the component classical RL depends on: it supplies the per-step baseline that makes credit assignment over a 300-step episode tractable. GRPO’s design assumptions (competent base model, short sequences, verifiable rewards) hold for LLM fine-tuning and not for a randomly initialized policy learning from sparse reward.
